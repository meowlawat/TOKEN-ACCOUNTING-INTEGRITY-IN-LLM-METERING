Abstract (1,571 characters, under the 1,920-character limit you specified; condensed
from the paper's own abstract -- every number below is copied verbatim from the paper,
nothing added or changed):

Metered AI-inference services stream computational value to the client before the charge
is settled, placing the usage-metering and billing boundary inside the security perimeter
of the AI-serving infrastructure itself. We study this boundary's integrity against an
authenticated client that seeks to pay less than it owes for inference it received.
Modeling a metered request as a lifecycle with a single integrity property, V(r) <= N(r),
we localize failures to three architectural placements: state synchronization (B0),
commitment timing (M1), and usage authority (M2). We verify the lifecycle exhaustively in
TLA+/TLC across ten configurations and four invariants, and validate it on a testbed
spanning three execution topologies, three accounting families, and a third-party serving
stack. An architecture that recounts usage accurately but bills the client-declared value
leaks 58.3% of delivered value in a controlled setting and 69.0% against a real serving
stack, while server-authoritative billing eliminates the leak; asynchronous settlement
reproduces over-serving under strictly sequential arrivals with no concurrency at all, so
the condition concerns whether authorization is atomically coupled to economic
commitment, not concurrency. The strongest defense against usage-authority manipulation
costs under 0.1% of end-to-end time with real inference. The contribution is a systematic
security analysis of deployed AI-serving infrastructure: B0 is a known baseline, and
M1/M2 systematize, formalize, and measure mechanisms with prior individual precedent.
