Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Accounting Integrity in Metered LLM-Serving
Infrastructure: Verified Conditions Against Client-Side Under-Payment," for publication in
Future Generation Computer Systems.

WHAT THE PAPER IS ABOUT

A metered computational service decides whether to admit work before it knows how much
work will be done, delivers the work incrementally, and commits the charge afterwards.
Between admission and commitment the service holds economic state that is authoritative
for billing but not yet durable, and that state is read and written by several
components: an authorization path, an execution path, and a settlement path that may run
asynchronously behind both. The question the paper asks is whether such a service can
deliver economically valuable computation without an equivalent durable economic
commitment, and what architectural conditions prevent that.

LLM inference serving is the workload we use to answer it, because it exhibits every
ingredient at once: progressive value delivery, quantity unknown at authorization time, a
runtime-produced multi-category usage vector, and asynchronous settlement. The paper does
not study the behaviour, robustness, or output safety of the language model itself.

WHY FGCS

The contribution is a distributed state-management result demonstrated on a modern cloud
workload, not an attack paper.

- Distributed accounting state. A metered request is modelled as a lifecycle with one
  integrity property, V(r) <= N(r), where V is value delivered and N is the net committed
  debit. Three failure mechanisms are localized to three distinct elements of that
  lifecycle: a shared-state transition (B0), a terminal path (M1), and a data-authority
  boundary (M2). B0 is a lost update in the sense of the classical isolation-level
  literature; the paper connects it to that literature rather than presenting it as new.

- The central systems result concerns settlement, not concurrency. We issue requests
  strictly sequentially, 20 ms apart, so that no two are ever in flight together, and
  over-serving still appears once the settlement delay exceeds the inter-arrival time.
  This forces a strictly more general condition than the one we began with: authorization
  must be atomically coupled to economic commitment on the path that authorizes service.
  Concurrency is one way to break that coupling; deferred settlement is another.

- Verification. The lifecycle is specified in TLA+ and checked exhaustively with TLC
  across ten configurations and four invariants, with every expected outcome declared in
  the checking script before the runs; all forty matched, over 27,526 distinct states.
  The model is finite, checks safety only, and abstracts away the database and the
  network. There is no mechanized refinement proof from specification to implementation,
  and the manuscript says so in those words.

- Empirical validation across deployment variation. The same architectures are measured
  across three execution topologies, three accounting families including an asynchronous
  event pipeline, and two independent inference data planes, one of which is third-party
  serving code (llama.cpp serving SmolLM2-135M-Instruct over real server-sent-event
  streaming). Integrity gates fail closed and are validated by fault injection, and a
  separate audit re-derives every reported quantity from raw data using code that imports
  nothing from the project.

CONTRIBUTION, STATED CONSERVATIVELY

We claim no novelty at the primitive level, and say so in the abstract, the introduction,
and the discussion. Relying on a client-supplied value in a security decision is CWE-807;
racing a shared counter is a classical web race; disconnect-related accounting gaps are
documented in deployed gateways. The contribution is the unified accounting model, the
architectural taxonomy, the machine-checked and empirical evidence, and the sufficiency
conditions, not a new attack or a new defense primitive.

PUBLIC EVIDENCE, AND WHAT WE DID NOT DO

Section 3.10 tabulates five public reports from independently developed metering gateways
in which the same architectural ingredient appears, including one in which the project's
own maintainers measured 254,347 completion tokens delivered and never billed in a single
interrupted stream. We reviewed only the published issue and pull-request pages. We
attempted nothing against any third-party system, we make no claim that any listed system
is exploitable, and two of the five reports are already fixed by their own maintainers.
Every measurement in the paper comes from our own local testbed.

ON LIMITATIONS

The formal model is finite and checks safety only; there is no mechanized refinement
proof; the asynchronous family is measured but not formally modelled; no commercial
provider was tested, by deliberate ethical constraint; and real-stack validation uses one
external serving stack and one small model on CPU.

One limitation deserves the editor's attention directly, because it bears on the paper's
central systems claim. Separation between authorization and settlement is temporal in our
experiments, not physical: all topologies ran on a single host under Docker Compose over
loopback, and the reconciliation delay is an injected parameter under our control rather
than a measured network path. We therefore establish that breaking the coupling is
sufficient to produce over-serving, and that the effect appears once the delay exceeds the
inter-arrival time; we do not establish what that delay is in a physically distributed
deployment. A two-machine replication is the obvious next experiment. It was not
performed, and the manuscript carries a dedicated limitation paragraph saying so rather
than leaving a reader to infer it.

We also withdraw a detectability claim made in an earlier version of this work, after our
own audit showed the instrumentation restated experimenter-assigned labels rather than
measuring evidence. That withdrawal is documented in the paper.

ARTIFACT AND ETHICS

The testbed, attack harness, defense implementations, TLA+ specification, and complete raw
data are publicly released and archived with a DOI. Every table and figure regenerates
from raw data; none is hand-edited. Every attack in the artifact is paired with a defense.
All experiments ran against a local testbed whose harness defaults to localhost and
contains no third-party endpoints. No credentials, real user data, or production billing
APIs were used, and this work produced no vulnerability findings against any third-party
or production system.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been
published previously. We declare no competing interests.
