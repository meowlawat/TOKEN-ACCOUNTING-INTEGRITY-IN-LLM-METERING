A NOTE WORTH YOUR JUDGEMENT, NOT MINE

The resume records a bug-bounty finding at IdeaPulley: "a critical API race condition
via Burp Suite Intruder, bypassing rate-limiting and credit-deduction logic -- a
business-logic flaw with direct financial impact."

That is, in this paper's own vocabulary, a B0-class credit-decrement failure found in a
live system under an authorized engagement. It is arguably the strongest motivation the
paper could have: the failure class is not hypothetical, and the author has seen one.

It was NOT added to the manuscript. Three reasons:

  1. The paper is frozen. Adding a real-world finding is a new claim, and new claims do
     not go into a frozen manuscript without a fresh evidence review.
  2. Section 14 currently states that this work produced no new vulnerability findings
     against any third-party or production system. That remains true -- the bug hunt was
     separate, authorized work -- but mentioning the finding without carefully rewording
     that section would read as a contradiction.
  3. Disclosing a specific named company's business-logic flaw in a journal paper needs
     that company's agreement, and possibly the bug-bounty programme's disclosure terms
     permitting it. That is not a call to make on the author's behalf.

If you want it in, it belongs in the introduction as motivation, it needs IdeaPulley's
written permission, and Section 14 needs rewording in the same pass. Say the word and it
can be done properly.
